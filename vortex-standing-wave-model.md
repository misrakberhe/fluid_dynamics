# The Vortex-Ring and Standing-Wave Model of Perception

*A cognitive model built on physical structures. Written to be readable with no prior knowledge of
either Jungian typology or fluid dynamics.*

**Status:** an internally consistent generative framework. Each piece was built to resolve a
specific tension raised by the previous one, and no part currently contradicts another. It is
interpretive rather than tested — a way of thinking that generates ideas and predictions, not a
validated theory. A few pieces are flagged as testable but unconfirmed where they arise.

---

## Orientation

### The cast

Jungian psychology divides mental activity into functions. This model concerns only the four
**perceiving** functions — the ones that take in and hold information, as opposed to the
**judging** functions that evaluate it. They are conventionally named by two letters:

- **Se** (extraverted sensing) — live contact with immediate external input.
- **Ni** (introverted intuition) — internally held, self-developing structure.
- **Ne** (extraverted intuition) — outward-branching possibility and framing.
- **Si** (introverted sensing) — internally held, stable reference.

You do not need to accept anything about typology for the model to be interesting. Treat the four
names as labels for four structurally distinct modes, and the physics as the actual content.

### The organizing insight

The model separates two levels that are easy to conflate:

|  | **Cause** — the raw input type | **Effect** — the structure it accumulates into |
|---|---|---|
| **Momentum axis** | **Se** — propagating pulses | **Ni** — a vortex ring |
| **Position axis** | **Ne** — positional framing | **Si** — a standing wave |

The crucial distinction is **cause versus accumulated structure**, and it runs *across* the
momentum/position divide rather than along it. Ni and Si are both effects; they sit on opposite
sides of the same divide.

And the two physical forms are exact mirrors of each other:

- A **vortex ring** has momentum and no fixed position.
- A **standing wave** has a fixed position and no net momentum.

That mirror relationship is the spine of the whole model.

# Part I — Se and Ni: the vortex ring

## What a vortex ring is

Blow a smoke ring. What you have made is a doughnut of rotating fluid that holds its shape and
travels forward under its own power — it is not being pushed along by anything once it exists. The
rotation sustains the forward motion, and the forward motion sustains the rotation.

Two properties matter for the model:

1. **It is self-propagating.** Once formed, it keeps going without further input.
2. **It has no fixed position.** It is a coherent, identifiable *shape* that is never anywhere in
   particular. You can point at it, but not at where it lives.

**Se** is the pulse — the push of air, the shear that creates rotation. **Ni** is the ring.

## Formation

**The formation number (a saturation limit).** A ring can only absorb so much circulation from the
pulse that makes it. Past that threshold, additional input does not deepen the core — it sheds
behind as unstructured turbulence. A single experience has a natural ceiling on how much it can
feed one insight; beyond that, more exposure is wasted rather than compounding.

**Pinch-off.** The discrete moment the ring detaches from the trailing jet still feeding it and
becomes autonomous. Before pinch-off, the structure is tethered to its source. After, it travels on
its own. This is the candidate for the moment a nascent intuition becomes a *portable* insight —
something you can carry into unrelated situations — rather than remaining stuck to the specific
experience that produced it.

## Propagation

**Self-induced velocity.** How fast a ring travels is governed by the **ratio** of core radius to
ring radius, not by size and speed as independent knobs. A thin-cored, wide ring behaves quite
differently from a thick-cored, tight one. This replaces a loose "bigger insights move slower"
intuition with a specific geometric relationship: shape determines speed.

## What is inside a ring

**Fluid parcels.** Instead of tracking the flow as a field, you can track small identity-persistent
chunks of fluid as they move. These map onto the sub-components of an experience — individual
remembered details, particular sensory threads — that get captured into a core as it rolls up.

This gives Ni a **two-level structure**:

- The **core** as an emergent rotating whole.
- The **parcels** as individually traceable constituents inside it.

And parcels are not equivalent:

- **Centre parcels** have been integrated so long that their motion is indistinguishable from the
  core's own. You cannot separate them out anymore; they *are* the insight.
- **Edge parcels** are newly captured, still separable, still strippable without damaging the
  structure.

This centre/edge gradient recurs later as a general principle.

## Complexity scaling

More parcels in play raises the system's effective **Reynolds number** — in fluid dynamics, the
ratio of inertial and shear effects to damping. High Reynolds number means turbulence dominates over
smoothing. Carried into the model, three consequences follow:

- **Interaction complexity rises combinatorially** with parcel count, not linearly.
- **The formation window narrows** — the range of technique parameters that still produces a clean
  core shrinks, leaving less margin for error.
- **Instability risk rises** alongside formation difficulty.

Net effect: **complex insights are both harder to form and more fragile once formed** — not because
complex ideas are inherently delicate, but because high parcel count pushes the system into a regime
where coherent structure is intrinsically harder to sustain.

## Failure modes

The model distinguishes three, which are genuinely different processes rather than degrees of the
same thing:

- **Viscous decay** — gradual fading. The structure dissipates through ordinary friction with its
  surroundings.
- **Widnall (azimuthal) instability** — a ring carrying too much energy relative to its core
  thickness develops a waviness around its circumference, then buckles and fragments into smaller
  rings. This is failure from **over-intensity or forcing**, not from neglect.
- **Reconnection** — two rings deliberately merging into one. Interactive rather than a failure at
  all, but it does end the original structures.

## Interactions between rings

**Leapfrogging.** Each ring's rotation induces a velocity field extending into the surrounding
space. When two rings are near each other, these fields overlap, and each affects the other's
motion — one accelerates through the other, they trade places, and repeat. This overlap is the
actual mechanism. It also means two structures can be dynamically coupled while appearing spatially
separate.

**Multiple pulses in sequence.** Closely timed related experiences can be captured into each other's
wake and merge or accelerate. This is leapfrogging's cause, and it suggests the effect can be
*manufactured* deliberately through timing rather than left to chance.

**Wall interaction.** A ring meeting a solid boundary does not penetrate it. It spreads radially and
decelerates, converting forward motion into expansion. This is the candidate model for a new idea
meeting a firmly held existing conviction: it flattens and spreads sideways without merging or
leapfrogging. Nothing is destroyed; nothing gets through either.

**Head-on collision.** Two rings travelling toward each other flatten on contact and can shed
smaller secondary rings at the collision plane. A distinct third interaction category, separate from
both leapfrogging and reconnection.

**External strain field.** Strain is the stretching-and-deforming component of a flow, as opposed to
its translation or rotation. A strain field acting on a ring thins and elongates the core — changing
its speed and stability, since those depend on the core-to-ring ratio — **without adding or removing
any rotation.** An influence that reshapes a structure's dynamics without contributing or taking
away content.

## Pulse engineering: the person as piston

If ring formation is engineerable, then generating pulses is an active design problem rather than
passive exposure. The reframe is that **you are the piston, not the fluid.** Controllable
parameters:

- **Stroke ratio** (pulse length versus opening size) — an experience needs to be *sized* correctly.
  Too short and it never rolls up into anything. Too long, past the formation number, and the excess
  is wasted as unstructured trailing exposure.
- **Velocity program** (the shape of the push over time) — front-loaded intensity produces a tighter,
  stronger core than even pacing. **First contact matters disproportionately.**
- **Orifice geometry** — unusually *shaped* experiences (atypical framing or format) may produce
  insights that keep re-presenting themselves differently over time rather than settling immediately.
- **Sequencing** — timing successive pulses to deliberately produce merging and acceleration.

**Open tension, unresolved:** does fully deliberate, optimised pulse generation lose something that
naturally chaotic, unplanned exposure provides — namely unexpected shear directions nobody would
have thought to design?

## The economics of learning

Getting a clean, reliable ring in a given setup requires finding a narrow working parameter window.
That is expensive by blind trial and error and cheap once someone has mapped it for you. This splits
the effort of learning into two components that behave differently:

- **Search cost** — finding which technique produces which structure. This is the expensive part,
  and it is mostly what a good teacher or mentor collapses for you.
- **Execution cost** — reliably performing a technique you have already been shown. Still requires
  practice, but is far more tractable than open-ended search.

Two distinct failure modes come from mismatch between your repertoire and your targets:

- **Narrow technique set, wide target range** — certain structures are simply unreachable. This is a
  *coverage gap*, not a search problem, and no amount of searching fixes it.
- **Wide technique set, no guidance on fit** — expensive live search every single time, despite
  owning all the necessary tools.

**Residual value of chaos:** even with good technique, unplanned exposure remains useful as a cheap
background search process that occasionally turns up parameter combinations no technique yet exists
for.

## Adding to an already-complex structure

A complex, non-uniform existing core has *less* margin to absorb new material than a simple one,
because it already sits closer to its own instability threshold. Three consequences:

- **Rate matters more than volume.** Slow addition lets the core re-equilibrate around each new
  parcel before the next arrives. Fast injection risks forcing sudden reorganisation or outright
  fragmentation.
- **Entry point matters.** New parcels should enter at the **edge** and migrate inward gradually.
  Injecting directly into the core centre — challenging the most load-bearing part of an existing
  structure — is far more destabilising than peripheral addition.
- **Predicted expert fragility.** Someone with a complex core should be *more* destabilised by fast
  contradictory input than a novice with a simple one. This is framed as a property of structural
  complexity rather than personal capacity. **Flagged as testable but unconfirmed.**

**Deliberate complexity reduction.** Proposed as a fifth category alongside formation, propagation,
interaction and decay: an expert may rationally *prune* secondary, less load-bearing parcels to
regain absorption capacity and updatability, then re-add nuance once re-stabilised. This is a
maintenance operation performed by the person, not something that happens to the structure
passively.

# Part II — Ne and Si: the standing wave

## The starting problem

Imagine a system of existing Ni-cores, and bracket out the pulses that generate them. With the
pulses set aside, it becomes natural to divide the surrounding space into positional frames, giving
each core a discrete, nameable identity by location. **This is Ne: identity by positional division.**

The problem this creates is structural, not incidental:

- **A vortex core's position is never static, even with no new pulses arriving.** Cores keep
  propagating under self-induction. So **any positional frame is stale the moment it is drawn**,
  because the thing it divides is a continuous process rather than a fixed set of objects.
- **Two cores that look spatially separate under a frame can be dynamically entangled** through
  overlapping velocity fields. The frame can misrepresent coupled structures as independent.

And there is a trap in it: **bracketing out the pulses is exactly what makes clean, dividable frames
possible in the first place** — but it also removes the only mechanism that would tell you when a
frame has gone stale, or when two "separate" identities are actually one coupled system.

## First resolution

For a system oriented around maximising Ni's strengths, the resolution is that Ne is best used
**retrospectively and in a disjointed, interlude-like fashion** — an occasional interpretive framing
applied after the fact, not a continuously running tracking mechanism. This avoids the staleness
problem by simply not asking positional framing to do a live-tracking job it structurally cannot do
well.

(This turns out to describe only Ne's *conscious* use. A separate continuous structural role appears
below, and the apparent contradiction is resolved in Part V.)

## Si needs a structure of its own

Si and Ni are both **introverted** perceiving functions — internally held structure rather than live
external contact. But Ni is active and propagating while Si is meant to be *stabilising*. So Si needs
its own strange structure, analogous in kind to a vortex core but different in behaviour. Three
candidates were explored:

- **Stratification.** Parcels settling into discrete layers over time, like sediment, accessed by
  depth-matching rather than propagation. Gives Si genuine retained content, but only as a static,
  discrete-retrieval model.
- **A steady tone.** If Se is a *frequency* — each pulse carrying its own transient character — then
  Si is a single, steady, unmodulated tone running continuously underneath. This produces **beating**:
  when two frequencies are close but not identical, their interference pulses slowly, and the beat
  rate is a natural continuous signal of match quality. Slow beat means close match, or familiarity.
  Fast beat means mismatch, or novelty.
- **A non-momentum shape.** The explicit structural mirror of Ni. Ni is a shape with momentum and no
  fixed position; Si is proposed as the inverse — a shape with fixed position and no net momentum.

## The unification

A **standing wave** satisfies all three at once, rather than forcing a choice between them.

**What a standing wave is:** two waves of the same frequency travelling in *opposite* directions,
interfering. The result is a fixed spatial pattern — nodes that never move, antinodes that oscillate
in place — with **zero net propagation**. A plucked guitar string is one. It is vigorously active and
going absolutely nowhere.

How it satisfies each candidate:

- **Non-momentum shape** — exactly. Fixed position, zero net momentum. The precise structural mirror
  of a vortex ring.
- **Steady tone** — a standing wave has a fundamental frequency. That *is* the tone.
- **Stratification** — a standing wave carries a stack of **harmonic overtones** riding on the
  fundamental simultaneously. This recovers the layering idea, but not as sequentially deposited
  sediment: as **harmonic layers of one coexisting structure.** All present at once, not stacked by
  age.
- **Beating** — still applies for near-but-different incoming frequencies.

This gives Si **two distinct interaction modes** with incoming Se, depending on match quality:

- **Comparison (beating)** — on mismatch. The beat rate reports how far off the new input is.
- **Reinforcement (construction)** — on exact match. A pulse at Si's own frequency does not beat
  against it; it reflects and *reinforces* the standing structure, building it further.

## The boundary problem

A standing wave needs a **reflecting boundary** — a cavity — to sustain itself. Without one it
dissipates outward. A guitar string is bounded at both ends by the nut and the bridge; that is what
makes standing waves possible on it at all.

**Ne's positional frames are that boundary.** Not supplying content to Si, but *containing* it —
giving the tone something to reflect off.

## The resulting asymmetry

This produces a real structural asymmetry between the two perceiving axes, worth holding as a named
distinction rather than treating the axes as clean mirror images:

- **Se → Ni: active material supply.** Se generates Ni's core directly, through ongoing shear.
- **Ne → Si: containment, not supply.** Ne does not feed Si content. It provides the boundary
  condition that lets Si's self-sustaining tone persist rather than dissipate.

And this explains something that would otherwise look arbitrary. Positional framing is a *poor* tool
for tracking a propagating Ni-core (Part II's starting problem) and a *good* tool for containing a
stationary Si-structure — because the frame does not need to update to track motion, since Si, once
bounded, is not going anywhere. **The same mechanism is mismatched to one perceiving function and
well-matched to the other, for structural reasons rather than by coincidence.**

There is also a shared property worth noting: **Si is not permanently stable once formed.** Like Ni,
it requires ongoing maintenance and reinforcement to persist.

## Ne and Se

Ne is not simply "the absence of Se" or a passive counterpart. It is its own distinct thing, standing
in a relationship to Se that is both **antithetical and analogous** — mirroring the same kind of
relationship Ni and Si have with each other (both introverted perceivers, opposite in behaviour —
active-propagating versus stable-contained — yet structurally parallel in kind).

This keeps all four relationships distinct rather than collapsing any into a restatement of another:
Se↔Ni within the vortex model, Ne↔Si within the standing-wave model, and Se↔Ne and Ni↔Si across the
two.

# Part III — The gating layer

**Importance does not live in the ring's shape or geometry.** It is a separate axis, sitting in the
*relations between* parcels — and it corresponds to the classically named **judging** functions, as
opposed to the perceiving functions that generate the ring mechanics themselves.

**Fi as a limit/gating function.** Proposed specifically as a *threshold* that determines whether a
pulse is allowed to reach and interact with a core **at all**, before any shear-based mechanics
happen. This is structurally different from a strain-type effect, which reshapes a ring that is
already interacting.

At its most granular level, Fi behaves as a **hard binary edge**: a pulse either clears the threshold
and reaches the core largely unmodified, or is blocked outright. That all-or-nothing signature is
what distinguishes a gating field from a continuous deformer. *Explicitly not fully explored — flagged
to revisit.*

**Speculative extension, not developed:** Ti as an internally-oriented logical-consistency gate; Te
and Fe as externally-oriented gates (efficiency and consequence versus social and relational impact).
Four gating fields on two axes — internal/external × logical/valuative — potentially conflicting on
the same incoming pulse.

# Part IV — A practical instantiation: reagents and reactions

This is confirmed as a *practical instance* of pulse generation rather than a parallel model —
specifically, what an Se-pulse looks like when zoomed into goal-directed action rather than general
experiential input.

**The natural mode it describes:** select one minimal, well-chosen action (the reagent) matched to the
current state of the situation (the solution), then let existing dynamics carry it toward the goal —
rather than continuously forcing progress by hand.

The mapping, carried directly across from the vortex mechanics:

- **Stroke ratio / formation number → dose size.** Too little and there is no reaction. Too much and
  you get uncontrolled side effects, or burnout.
- **Front-loaded velocity program → a sharp, well-chosen first move,** then letting kinetics carry it,
  rather than sustained even pressure.
- **Shear needing contrast → activation energy.** A reagent only reacts against a primed solution. The
  real skill is **reading whether the situation is in a state to react** — not having a bigger reagent.
- **Slow parcel addition to complex cores → titration.** The caution logic carries over unchanged.
- **Catalyst** — a new category with no clean vortex equivalent: an action that changes how *future*
  actions react, without itself being a step toward the goal.
- **False equilibrium** — a pursuit can stall in a stable-but-wrong state. Escaping it may require a
  *different* reagent than the one that produced initial progress, not more of the same.

**Proposed sequence:** (1) read the current state before acting; (2) choose the single action most
likely to clear activation energy *given that state* — not the biggest or most obviously goal-aligned
one; (3) let it run without over-intervening; (4) if stalled, diagnose for false equilibrium rather
than pushing harder with the same action.

## Acquisition versus application

A practical refinement: users following this reagent-based pattern tend to either skimp on acquiring
new reagents or get overwhelmed by too many options — because **acquisition (search) and application
(live goal-pursuit) are being done simultaneously.** That splits attention and forces either
over-reliance on familiar reagents or expensive live comparison of untested ones.

The fix is separating them into two modes, done at different times:

- **Acquisition mode.** No active goal in play. Deliberately trying new techniques, low-stakes
  practice. The output is *shelf-stock* — a wider range of known, tested reagents — not goal progress.
  This is where search cost gets paid deliberately, outside the pressure of live application.
- **Application mode.** Active goal in play. Select only from the already-stocked shelf; do not attempt
  to learn or invent mid-attempt. This keeps the "sharp minimal action, then don't stir" behaviour
  clean.

**Open question, deferred:** whether acquisition mode should be unguided (broad exploration) or guided
(curriculum-style, pointed at known-useful techniques) — or needs both, with guided acquisition for
known-useful reagents plus unguided time preserving the cheap background search for unusual shears.

# Part V — Conscious versus unconscious operation

An apparent contradiction arose within the model. Ne was described as occasional and retrospective
(Part II's first resolution), but *also* as a continuously present cavity wall sustaining Si (Part II's
boundary problem). Both cannot be true of the same thing at the same level.

**The resolution is to separate structural activity from conscious activity:**

- **All four functions run continuously as background structural processes** — Ne holding containment,
  Si holding tone, Ni holding cores, Se generating pulses — regardless of whether the person is
  consciously attending to any of them.
- **Only a thin edge of that ongoing activity ever surfaces into conscious awareness** at a given
  moment.

That second point deliberately reuses the centre/edge parcel distinction from Part I, now applied to
function-activity generally rather than just to content inside a core. Long-integrated activity is
indistinguishable from the ongoing process itself; only the newly-active edge is separately
identifiable.

**This reframes the function stack.** Dominant, auxiliary, tertiary and inferior ordering describes
**how readily or how often a function's edge-activity surfaces to conscious awareness** — not whether
the function is active at all. All four are always running underneath.

And it resolves the contradiction directly: **Ne's containment of Si runs continuously and
unconsciously; Ne's occasional, disjointed, retrospective framing of Se-pulses is the separate, sparse,
conscious edge of that same underlying function.**

**Important scoping note.** Si running largely unconsciously — with low conscious edge-access — applies
specifically to Si as a comparatively weak, low-stack function in an Ni-dominant person. This is not a
universal claim about Si. For someone where Si is dominant or auxiliary, its edge-activity would be
expected to surface far more readily, the way Ni's does for an Ni-dominant person. **Conscious versus
unconscious status is a function of stack position and strength, not a fixed property of any particular
function.**

# Summary of the architecture

| Element | Physical form | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Se** | A pulse or shear | Propagating input; generates structure directly |
| **Ni** | A vortex ring | Self-propagating, momentum, no fixed position; holds parcels |
| **Ne** | A positional frame | Divides space into nameable identities; contains rather than supplies |
| **Si** | A standing wave | Fixed position, zero net momentum; fundamental tone plus harmonics |
| **Fi** | A hard threshold gate | Binary admit/block, applied before any shear mechanics |

**The two failure-mode families:** Ni fails by fading (viscous decay), by fragmenting under
over-intensity (Widnall instability), or by merging (reconnection). Si fails by dissipating when its
boundary is absent, or by decaying without reinforcement.

**The two shapes are mirrors.** Momentum without position; position without momentum. Same underlying
wave material, different boundary conditions — a ring left free to propagate, a tone confined and
reflecting on itself.

**The one thing the model does not yet do:** it has not been reframed into falsifiable, measurable
claims. That remains a long-horizon undertaking rather than a near-term project. What it does provide
is a differentiated vocabulary in which specific, non-obvious predictions — expert fragility, the
narrowing formation window, entry-point sensitivity — can at least be *stated* precisely enough to be
tested later.
